Keyboard: ←/→ move, ↑ or X rotate clockwise, Z or Shift rotate counter-clockwise, ↓ soft drop, Space hard drop, Esc pause (hides the board). Touch: drag sideways to move, tap to rotate, swipe down to drop. NES rules: 40/100/300/1200 points for 1/2/3/4 lines, × (level+1); the level rises every 10 lines and the pieces fall faster — a tetris pays 30× a single.

About the speed: level 29 is the last speed-up — real NES gravity is one row per frame from 29 all the way to 255, so it never gets faster again. Starting at 18, you reach level 19 at 130 lines and level 29 at 230; those line counts are what people usually remember as “130 and 230”. The faster-than-29 screens in CTWC Masters matches come from a ROM hack, not the original game — that is the CTWC 2×ks option above, where the piece drops two rows per step from level 39.

All the players run right here in your browser, on the exact engine the AI trained against. Dellacherie is the classic six-feature hand-tuned evaluator; Trained net is a masked dueling DQN choosing among all ~23 possible landings of each piece; the search tiers add a look-ahead over the next piece. Rising garbage is the honest benchmark — it separates the players fast.

Dellacherie + search plays by default, not the trained net — because on the one skill that matters most it is simply better. Around 51% of the lines it clears come from tetrises, against 18% for plain Dellacherie and under 3% for the net, which clears plenty of lines but burns them one at a time. The net learns to copy the same evaluator, and copying it imperfectly is enough to lose the tetrises: keeping a well open needs about ten correct decisions in a row, and one wrong one fills it in. Pick Trained net above to watch the difference.

Input technique limits how fast the AI can move a piece sideways, the same way it limits a human. DAS holds the d-pad and waits 16 frames for the ROM to start auto-shifting, then gets one shift every 6 (10 Hz). Hypertapping taps by hand — no waiting, about 12 Hz. Rolling, invented in 2020, rolls the fingers against the back of the controller for about 20 Hz, and it is the reason modern scores dwarf the pre-2020 records. Try starting at level 29 and switching between them: DAS cannot get a piece to the far wall before it lands, so it stops being able to feed the well at all — the AI knows this and stops trying to build one.
